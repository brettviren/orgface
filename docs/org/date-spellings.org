[[file:command-log.org][org log]] and [[file:command-visit.org][org visit]] take dates in the forms people actually type at
=git log=. This is a *documented subset* of git's =approxidate=, not a
reimplementation of it: anything unrecognised is reported rather than
guessed at.

* Forms

- Absolute :: =2026-07-30=, =2026/07/30=, =20260730=, =260730=, each
  optionally with a time — =2026-07-30 14:05= or =2026-07-30T14:05:00=.
- Named :: =today=, =yesterday=, =now=.
- Relative :: =3 days ago=, =2 weeks ago=, =1 month ago=, and git's
  dotted =2.weeks.ago=. Units may be plural and =ago= may be left off.
- Last :: =last week=, =last month=, =last year=.

Units run from =second= through =minute=, =hour=, =day=, =week=,
=fortnight=, =month= and =year=, with the short spellings =s=, =min=, =h=,
=d= and =w=.

* Whole-day bounds

An upper bound named as a bare day covers that whole day: =--until
2026-07-30= includes everything written on the 30th, rather than everything
before midnight at its start. A lower bound named the same way starts at
midnight, which is the same rule read from the other end.

* Why a subset

git's =approxidate= accepts a great deal and guesses at the rest. Guessing
is fine for a tool you are watching interactively and bad for one whose
output you might act on: =org log --since "next tuesdya"= should say it
does not understand rather than quietly report the wrong fortnight.
